Questions we answer

Each question links to the simulator that shows it best.

Why do we have day and night?

Because the Earth turns once a day, each place moves in and out of the sunlit side. Play the globe simulator and watch a city travel from the bright side to the dark side.

Open → Globe 3D Sun Simulator

Why do the seasons change?

The Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.44°, so the latitude where sunlight arrives most directly shifts north and south through the year. Move the date slider to the equinoxes and solstices.

Open → Earth Orbit SimulatorOpen → Globe 3D Sun Simulator

Why does the sun's height differ from city to city on the same day?

Each city has a different latitude and longitude, so the local time and the angle to the sun differ at the same moment. Switch cities and compare sun elevation and day length.

How do midnight sun and polar night happen?

Because of the axial tilt, the polar regions see the sun all day in summer and not at all in winter. Set the globe to the view from above the North Pole and move the date.

Is Earth closer to the Sun in summer?

No. Earth is closest to the Sun in early January and farthest in early July. The seasons come from the tilt of the axis, not from distance. Move the date in the orbit simulator and watch the distance.

Open → Earth Orbit Simulator

How does the Moon move around the Sun?

The Moon circles Earth in about 27.3 days while travelling with Earth around the Sun. Seen from the Sun, its path never loops and only curves gently. Switch on the Moon path in the simulator. The Moon's distance is exaggerated.
